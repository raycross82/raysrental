Yes, We Drive to Ellis County

Waxahachie sits further out than most DFW rental companies are willing to go, which is why so many phone calls from here end the same way — a polite apology, or a quote with a long-distance line item bolted onto it. We put the city in writing instead of hiding it inside “and surrounding areas.” Ellis County is a route we drive, not an exception we make.

What comes off the truck is seating, not spectacle: six-foot folding tables, white resin chairs, a $12 cooler for when you would rather guests stopped opening your refrigerator, and a $30 JBL PartyBox for the toast or the playlist that has to reach the far end of the yard. No inflatables, no tents. The trade is depth instead of breadth — a short catalogue means we know precisely what is on the rack and what shape it is in.

Graduations, quinceañeras, church socials, reunions, and the birthday that went from twelve guests to forty inside a week: that is the Waxahachie order book. If your headcount is still moving, the seating guide turns a guest list into a table number without the guessing.

Ten Pavilions, Six Parks, and a Working Outlet

For a city this size, Waxahachie is unusually well stocked with rentable pavilions. Getzendaner Park has three on its own, and the city rents more at Chapman Park (two), Lee Penn Park (two), Brown-Singleton Park, Lions Park and Oak Lawn Park (one apiece). Call it roughly ten rentable pavilions across six parks — an actual choice of venue rather than one shelter the whole town competes for.

Getzendaner is the one most people picture: 33 acres along Waxahachie Creek with a playground, shade trees and access to the city's hike and bike trail system. The historic Chautauqua Auditorium stands in the park as well, so the setting does some of the decorating for you.

Now the detail worth planning around. The pavilions at Getzendaner include electricity and restrooms, and electricity quietly changes what an outdoor party can be. It means a powered speaker rather than a battery that fades halfway through the dancing. It means slow cookers holding food at temperature for four hours instead of foil pans going lukewarm on a folding table. It means string lights when the evening runs long, and a phone charger for whoever is running the music. If one feature decides which pavilion you book, make it that one.

Pavilion rentals can be booked online through the city's system, so you can hold a date without a counter visit or a phone queue. Reserve the pavilion first, then send us the park, which pavilion you have, and the hours it covers. One more thing before you give us a number: count the fixed picnic tables already sitting under the roof. You only need to rent the difference, and that is the cheapest correction you will ever make to an order.

Waxahachie FAQs

Good to Know

Do you charge extra to deliver to Waxahachie?
Delivery is priced by your location, the same way it is priced everywhere else we go. Ellis County is part of our regular coverage rather than a special trip, and pickup at our DFW lot is free if you'd rather skip the delivery line entirely.
Can you deliver to a pavilion at Getzendaner Park?
Yes. Book the pavilion through the city's online reservation system, then send us the park, which pavilion you have and your hours. Getzendaner's pavilions include electricity and restrooms, which matters if you're running a speaker or slow cookers.
How many park pavilions can I rent in Waxahachie?
Roughly ten across six parks: three at Getzendaner Park, two each at Chapman Park and Lee Penn Park, and one each at Brown-Singleton Park, Lions Park and Oak Lawn Park. That's more choice than most towns this size can offer.
